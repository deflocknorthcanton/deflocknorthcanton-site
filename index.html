But this means Council never got asked. No mayor signed either contract, and the current mayor took office in 2025, two years after the renewal. Nobody in that building today chose this. It just never had to be a public decision.

who owns the data

On paper the City owns it. In practice it controls almost none of it.

The contract is clear on ownership: "all right, title and interest in the Agency Data, belong to and are retained solely by Agency." contract Here is what that gets us.

  • Does the City hold the data? NO "does not host servers for ALPR generated data" · NCPD Policy 428.4
  • Does the City decide how long it's kept? NO retention "is subject to the ALPR software company's data retention policy" · Policy 428.4
  • Does the City own the devices? NO "shall remain the personal property of Flock" · 2022 agreement
  • Does the City even have a copy of the rules? NO the signed renewal doesn't contain its own legal terms. It points to a page on Flock's website, and Flock rewrote that page in February 2026, two years after we signed
  • Do Flock's rights end when the contract does? NO rights run "during and after the Term hereof" · §4.5

Our police department wrote it in its own policy manual. It does not hold this data and does not set the clock on it. The company does. policy

And they can train AI on it. §4.5 lets Flock anonymize what our devices collect and use it "including the training of machine learning algorithms," under a right that runs "during and after the Term hereof." We can stop paying, the devices can come down, and that part continues. To be fair: the same contract bars Flock from selling it, three separate times. contract

why I think you should care

Where you've been doesn't stay in North Canton

Flock's own product calls it "license plate location history." The department's network export, dated June 2026, lists 1,944 organizations. North Canton's network is shared with 1,920 of them. the raw file

Names in that file reference all fifty state codes. State police in New York, Indiana and Idaho. The Ohio Attorney General's office. Drug task forces. Campus police from Philadelphia to Virginia. Three entries carry Flock's own [Federal] label:

  • [Federal] Wright Patterson OH Air Force Base
  • [Federal] US Postal Inspection Service
  • [Federal] Indiana Dunes National Park IN PD

The spreadsheet is linked above, and you can scroll it yourself.

Ohio cities are already hitting the brakes

82 Flock contracts have been cancelled across 28 states since 2021, thirty nine of them in the first five months of 2026. It's not a fringe position, and it's not just far away:

  • Dayton, OH · Audited its Flock logs and found 7,100 searches citing immigration purposes, which its own policy banned. Program suspended, cameras bagged, independent audit underway. Their police chief's words: "It's not a system issue, it's an us issue." WOSU
  • Cleveland, OH · Council voted to switch its 100 cameras off, then extended six months while it decides. Either way: they're debating it, in public, right now. Cleveland 19
  • Kent, OH · Looked at a Flock contract and said no thanks. Signal Ohio
  • Columbus, OH · After an audit of who was searching its cameras, the Mayor ordered police to stop statewide sharing entirely, July 2026. WOSU
  • Dane County, WI · Board voted 32 to 1 to cut the $80,000 from its budget, April 2026 WisPolitics
  • Oak Park, IL · Village Board voted 5 to 2 to cancel, August 2025 Wednesday Journal
  • Cambridge, MA · Terminated for "breach of trust" after Flock installed devices the city never authorized Cambridge Day
  • Austin, TX · Ended the program June 2025. Every device on city land came down. KUT

Every one of those cities did something we haven't done yet: talked about it in public.

common questions

The questions I had too

Don't these help catch criminals?

Sometimes, genuinely. Stolen cars, missing people. This isn't about whether our police are good or bad.

$51,167.81 for five surveillance devices we don't own, on a system we don't control, shared with nineteen hundred organizations, part of a national network, all decided without one public meeting… isn't worth renewing. You might read the same records and land somewhere else. That's fair.

Has anyone broken the rules here?

Not that I've found. I read every document the City gave me and found no evidence anyone at North Canton PD has misused this system.

What I can tell you is what the system has allowed elsewhere. The Electronic Frontier Foundation reviewed 12 million Flock searches across 3,900 agencies. They found searches tied to protests, searches using ethnic slurs, and a Texas deputy who searched 83,000 cameras looking for a woman who'd had an abortion. EFF Separate reporting has tracked officers using it on women: a Georgia police chief was arrested after women seeking protective orders said he used Flock to follow them, a Tennessee officer was indicted on 23 counts, and a Missouri officer ran one woman's plate nearly 400 times. reporting

That happened in departments that were also confident it wouldn't.

Don't the audit logs prevent misuse?

Every search is logged with a stated reason, and our department points to that as its safeguard.

But every case of abuse also happened in a system with audit logs. Logs record misuse after the fact, and somebody has to read them. In Dayton that took seven months and 7,100 searches.

How long do they keep it?

The order form says 30 days, and the 2022 contract made it a promise: "Flock will automatically delete Footage older than thirty (30) days." contract

But deleted doesn't mean gone. The same contract lets Flock "store deleted Footage in order to comply with certain legal obligations," with no time limit stated anywhere.

And the terms in force today aren't in the contract at all. They live on a Flock web page, rewritten in February 2026, two years after we signed. That version contains no promise to delete anything.

Aren't there only five of them?

Five here. But the surveillance network is the product. North Canton's devices feed a system our department shares with nineteen hundred other organizations, and their devices feed back.

Dane County, Wisconsin had twenty four devices shared with more than a hundred and forty agencies. They cut theirs anyway. WisPolitics

Do we even own the devices?

No. "Flock Hardware shall remain the personal property of Flock." contract After $51,167.81 the City owns none of them. The flip side is good news: when the contract ends, Flock takes them down at no cost to us.

the exit clause

It ends with one letter

The contract doesn't have to be broken, bought out, or fought over. There's an easy out, written into the contract itself.

"This Agreement will automatically renew … unless either Party gives the other Party notice of non-renewal at least thirty (30) days prior to the end of the then-current term."

2023 renewal contract · read it ↗

Thirty days' written notice before the term ends. No lawsuit, no buyout, no penalty. And Flock removes the hardware "at no additional cost to Agency." contract
